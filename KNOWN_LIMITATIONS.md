# Known Limitations

## Current build

- **Only tested on Linux.** The editor is built on cross-platform pieces (Electron, Three.js) but has not yet been run on Windows or macOS. Packaged installers come in Phase 9.
- **Rotating or scaling a group re-projects world-aligned textures** on the brushes inside it (moving keeps them locked). Non-uniform scale on a rotated parent is stored as the nearest rotation and scale (no shear).
- **No box selection** in the viewport yet.
- **Lights are markers only.** They don't light the scene until materials and lighting land (Phase 7).
- **Scenes** can be added and opened but not yet renamed, deleted or reordered from the editor. A level change doesn't remember the state of the scene left behind (going back starts it fresh), and moving objects don't travel with the player. Screens and items belong to each scene, so a game with several scenes needs them in each.
- **The clipboard is inside the editor.** Copy/paste works across projects within one session, but not with other apps or after a restart.
- **Exported games:** folders rather than installers; programs aren't signed, so Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper warn on first start (on macOS: right-click › Open); 64-bit Intel/AMD builds for other systems (Apple Silicon Macs run them through Rosetta); web games need a web server. Exporting for another system needs the internet once. The world is kept only when Game rules › Keep the world is on; “When the game starts” blocks run again in a saved game (copies they make at once are matched up, not doubled, but later moves or copies they make happen again), trains start from their first point, and a held object is dropped where it was.
- **Play as the player needs a Player start** (new scenes have one). Without it, Play is the physics preview with the free camera. Lighting is real-time (no baked lightmaps yet) and bulb shadows are costly, so use them sparingly; the player has no body or weapon model.
- **Riding physics objects:** only point objects inside a physics object ride with it (brushes inside stay put). Copies made by “Make a copy” don't bring their lights. A carried sun doesn't move.
- **Pivots apply to one selected object.** With several selected, the gizmo uses the middle of them all; a pivot set on a brush stays put relative to it when moved, but not when it is rotated through its parent group.
- **Health and score are simple:** one player with 100 health and up to 100 armour, no lives or game-over screen; chargers don't fill up again or make a sound; dying starts again at the Player start straight away, and nothing that moved is put back. The score isn't saved between plays. The console can only be read after Esc frees the mouse.
- **Game values:** renaming one doesn't update the blocks that use it (they show a problem until re-picked), and changes to game values are saved at once and can't be undone with Ctrl+Z. There is no countdown or progress widget yet; show a value instead.
- **Text signs** don't move with an object a script moves, and Show/Hide don't apply to them yet.
- **Copies:** a copy is one body: brushes inside a copied object with Physics are left out unless they have Physics too, text signs inside copies aren't drawn, and trigger zones inside a copy stay where they were made. Only objects in the scene can be copied (hide one to use it as a template); prefab files can't be copied directly yet.
- **Object actions:** a Scale and a Move on the same object at once replace each other (the newer one wins); point objects' collision shapes are rescaled, but their drawn shape isn't. Breakable objects break only from the Damage block (no damage from hits or falls yet), and a broken object stays in the game, invisible and not colliding, so its “When this breaks” stacks can finish.
- **Third-person camera:** a simple capsule body, no animation; Use still aims from the player's eyes.
- **Assets:** GLB files and glTF files with embedded data only (a .gltf with a separate .bin or image files can't be imported yet). Models don't animate in the editor, and models inside copied brush objects aren't drawn. Lighting is real-time; there are no baked lightmaps.
- **Moving platforms carry the player's feet, not their view:** on a turning platform the player goes round with it but keeps facing the same way. While a lift moves the player can't jump.
- **The player is an upright cylinder** (radius 16, 72 tall) rather than Source's box, so they slide round pillars and balls; they also step off a ledge a little sooner at its corners.
- **Screen UI:** one font, no animations or transitions, and no text input boxes; screens belong to the scene (a second scene would need its own); lists hold text rows or the inventory (not other data).
- **Creatures** placed inside something (a floor higher than where they were put) rise until they fit when the game starts. They don't animate in the editor (only while playing), and find their way only round what is near (they steer round crates, pillars and each other, and give up on spots they can't reach; finding a way through a maze of rooms comes with navigation meshes in A2), don't take cover, open doors or climb ladders, and can't be moved, shown or hidden by the usual blocks. Dead creatures stay where they fell. A model's animations play one at a time (no blending).
- **Hunter mind**: spots have no doors of their own, so “entrance open” means someone got in or out in the last moment; a door that should swing is the spot's own script (“When a creature opens a hiding spot”). Moves last a set time (Moves, or a default), not the length of the animation clip. A Hunter's hearing ignores what the sound is (only how far it carries). One player only (the engine has no multiplayer). Hiding spots are checked from in front of where their Look from object looks; a brush spot without one is checked from its north side.
- **Hiding spots**: the spot's view and front are worked out from where it starts (a spot moved by scripts keeps its first place for creatures).
- **Googly eyes** go on point objects only (not brushes), and their pupils react to how the object moves, not to hits or sounds. On a model other than the chicken they are placed by hand (Position). They ride on one bone as a whole (not bent by several, like skin), and only in Play: models don't animate in the editor.
- **Trains** go in straight lines between points (no curves), turn only left and right (not up slopes), and snap to their first point when the game starts. A train inside another moving object doesn't run. Path points can't be moved by scripts.
- **Particles** only show while playing (not in the editor), aren't lit or fogged, and can't use your own images yet. Marks are one round shape (no blood, no texture of your own); there are no decals you can place by hand (use a thin brush painted with an image).
- **Blasts light their surroundings two at a time:** more at once still show their fireball, without its light.
- **Weapons:** there's no weapon model in the player's hands, no reloading (ammo is used straight from the inventory), and no muzzle flash or recoil. Bullets can't hit the player yet (nothing but blasts shoots back until creatures arrive). Weapons belong to the scene's items, like other items. Clicks closer together than a weapon's rate are ignored.
- **Ladders and water** are fixed in place (scripts can't move them, so no rising water yet). Water's surface is the top of its brush (keep the top flat), there is no air meter on screen, health lost to drowning doesn't come back on its own, and the player can't throw objects into water to make splashes (no splash sounds or particles yet).
- **Player speeds are fixed** (Half-Life 2's: run 190, walk 100, crouch 64 u/s); a per-scene setting comes later.
- **Keys while playing are fixed** (WASD, Space, Ctrl or C, Shift, E); rebinding comes later. While the mouse is captured, editor shortcuts are off; F5 still stops.
- **Scripts:** blocks are rearranged with ↑ ↓ and copy and paste for now; drag-and-drop comes later. A message counts as “stopped coming” after 0.1 s without it, so a loop that sends it less often than that starts and stops it each time. The graph view (V3) comes later in Phase 5.
- **Applying a change to a prefab writes the prefab file straight away.** Undo restores this scene's copies but not the file; apply the old value again to change the prefab back.
- **Prefabs are edited through their copies.** Opening a prefab on its own to edit it, and prefabs inside prefabs, come later.
- **Brushes with Physics move as one solid piece** and aren't cut by subtract brushes while playing.
- **Edited meshes (moved into a dent or twist) aren't cut, clipped, merged or hollowed** until they are convex again, and can't be subtract brushes. Moving ones collide as their convex hull.
- **Loop cut runs across four-sided faces only;** it stops at triangles and other shapes.
- **The vertex tool moves corners with the gizmo only;** dragging a corner handle directly is not supported yet.
- **Materials:** plain materials have one image, one bump map and one glow (graph materials have every map); glowing faces don't light what's around them; reflections are the sky's colours, not the level around them; scroll speeds assume the usual face scale (0.25). Changes to materials are undoable, but they mark the scene as edited too. Models can take project materials per part; their images then follow the model's own texture coordinates (no triplanar mapping until CM2).
- **Surfaces made by a live cut** (the inside of a doorway) follow the wall around them and can't be selected until the cut is applied.
- **Big levels update a moment after an edit.** From 64 brushes the level is rebuilt in a background worker, so the view can lag an edit by the build time (shown in the stats). Picking uses the last finished build.
- **Brush rotation re-projects textures** (world alignment). Texture lock is exact for moves only.
- **Carving can drop hair-thin slivers** (< 0.5 u thick, ≤ 8 cubic units) left by cutters at very shallow angles; the count is reported.
- **Exported materials are only approximate outside CocoaWorlds** for wear and bevelled edges (they depend on CocoaWorlds' brush data). Maps are 8-bit, and graphs evaluated on another graphics card match only within a tolerance (mean under 1/255, largest under 8/255). `.cocoamat` files carry no `preview.png` yet, and importing a file whose material id is already in the project makes a copy rather than offering to replace it.
- **Automated end-to-end tests run on a software renderer** (SwiftShader). Real-GPU behaviour is covered by manual testing only so far.

## Expected limitations of the approved architecture

- **Exported game size.** Desktop games carry an Electron runtime (Chromium), so even a tiny game is roughly 100 MB.
- **macOS distribution.** Signing and notarization need a Mac and an Apple Developer ID. Unsigned macOS builds show a Gatekeeper warning.
- **Windows distribution.** Unsigned builds show a SmartScreen warning until a code-signing certificate is used.
- **Geometry.** Brushes are convex; edited meshes (G4) can take any closed shape but don't take part in carving. Carve results are several convex brushes. Arbitrary mesh booleans on imported models are not planned for v1.
- **Level scale.** No visibility precompilation (PVS); very large, dense levels rely on frustum culling and batching.
- **Sound areas.** Six built-in effects only (no custom echo settings); the area is the space inside all of the brush's faces, so draw it convex (an edited concave mesh counts as less than its shape); the area is found from the player's ears (the camera), not per sound; sounds from blocks and weapons can't opt out.
- **Web export.** Audio, including the background sound, waits for the first click (browser autoplay rules); mouse look needs a click to lock the pointer; large assets download before play.
- **Custom code.** No user JavaScript in v1; behaviour is built with blocks.
- **Platforms.** The editor targets desktop and laptop screens only.

## Fixed during development

- Newer Chromium returns a Promise from `scrollIntoView`; an effect that returned it implicitly crashed the editor when the console updated. Effects now never return values implicitly.
- Line-segment grids disappeared near the camera on software renderers; the grid is now drawn by a shader on a single plane.
- **Cocoa Materials:** wear on models is estimated from how the surface curves (flat-shaded boxes show none); wear images are always projected (triplanar); Flood fill (random per island) isn't there yet; triplanar mapping blends
  three projections, so very busy patterns can look doubled on slopes; bumps from a Height output (without a
  Normal output) follow face coordinates even when triplanar. Nodes can't be grouped into frames yet, and copied nodes stay inside the editor (not the system clipboard). Curve has four fixed points. Height to
  occlusion is a quick approximation. Graph files keep the name they were made with. Graphs are evaluated
  only in the editor; changing one from another workspace with Undo bakes it again in the background.
- **Ambient occlusion** is screen space (it only knows what is on screen) and is off with software rendering, so the automated tests (which render in software) don't check its look. Bounce light is not there yet (see docs/proposals/lighting-gi.md).
- **Repeat** copies are made when the scene is shown or played, so they can't be picked or edited one by one (a click picks the group), the selection outline covers only the parts, and a segment can't be cut short at the end (whole or stretched only). A model segment needs Step set, as only brushes are measured. Copies of a segment with a script don't run it.
- **Navigation** is measured once, when play starts (about a second on a 4096 × 4096 level): doors and moving platforms don't count as floor or wall, so creatures walk to a closed door and push against it. It's measured for a person-sized creature (32 wide, 72 tall); smaller ones still won't squeeze through gaps narrower than that, and bigger ones can try gaps too small. In the editor's view, models with collision aren't measured (in the game they are).
- **Collision meshes** (Auto): models drawn together as a batch (4 or more still copies) don't show their outlines with View › Show collisions. An animated collision mesh is moved after the physics step, so it trails the drawn model by one frame; it pushes nothing out of its way, and creatures' navigation still sees it where it started. Weighted (skinned) collision meshes stay in their rest pose: parent them to a bone instead.
- **Foliage** is grass tufts only, on the top of brushes: no models or custom plants yet, no flowers or rocks, nothing on models or on the sides of a brush, and no collision (it's purely visual). Each brush gets at most 20 000 tufts. The tufts are placed when the scene is shown or played, so they aren't in the saved file.
- **Many objects**: batching is in Play and exported games only; the editor still draws each model copy by itself (it needs each one to pick and outline). Animated models, models with bones, moving or breakable objects and objects a script names are drawn one by one.
- **Terrain** is edited in the editor only (players can't dig yet). Every terrain is the same size (2048 × 768 × 2048 u at a 16 u grid); a copy made with Duplicate shares the original's shape file. Its shape is saved with the scene, not by autosave. A heightmap stretches over the whole terrain. Layer textures use the material's colour picture only (not its bumps or roughness). Terrains are drawn chunk by chunk, so a big one costs a few hundred draw calls. Grass on a terrain is placed again a moment after each stroke; at most 80 000 tufts per terrain. Terrain tiles (T2) join only unturned terrains of the same size, a whole tile apart at the same height; every tile is loaded at once (no streaming yet); layers set differently on neighbouring tiles change sharply at the seam; a heightmap imported onto one tile leaves its edges unmatched until a stroke along the seam stitches them.
- **3D printers**: a print is cut by height only, so overhangs appear in mid-air as the layers reach them; the head's fill is a zig-zag over the layer's box, not its true inside. Printers are placed once (a printer moved by a script or physics leaves its prints where it was). Materials with raw shaders aren't cut (drawn whole while printing). A print made by the start blocks isn't saved as under way (the start blocks print it again). A print casts no shadow and gets no ambient occlusion until it is done. Models inside a copied or printed prop with Physics ride it but don't collide by their own shape; particles and effects inside copies aren't drawn; at most two lit copies of each light (eight in all) shine at once, and suns inside copies don't. There's no ready-made shop screen yet: build one with Screens and the Print block.
- **Base building** finds rooms on one storey (the ground plan); pieces go on grid edges only and are drawn as boxes; build sites exist only while playing and in saved progress (not editable in the editor). With saving on sleep (the default), leaving a scene keeps its base only as of the last sleep.
